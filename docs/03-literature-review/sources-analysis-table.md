# Sources Analysis Table

Structured analysis of the 12 sources used in the [literature review](literature-review.md). All sources are peer-reviewed, published in 2021–2026 and available in open access.

Hypotheses H1–H4 are defined in the [project proposal](../01-proposal/project-proposal.md).

| # | Source | Year | Type | Aim | Method & sample | Key findings | Limitations | Relevance to the project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Huang, Jew & Qi — *Heliyon* | 2023 | Systematic review | Identify MOOC engagement patterns and dropout factors in higher education | 21 studies (2008–2021) from 7 databases, PRISMA | Engagement develops in start, middle and end stages; dropout factors: course attributes, social status, cognitive ability, emotional factors (incl. motivation), learning behaviour (incl. early activity) | English-only studies; study authors not contacted; limited resources | H1, H2 |
| S2 | Rõõm, Lepp & Luik — *Education Sciences* | 2021 | Empirical, quantitative | Find when dropout peaks in programming MOOCs and how dropouts performed before leaving | 4 runs of 3 MOOCs, University of Tartu; 3,863 learners who started | Dropout highest in the first 1–2 weeks; completion fell from 60.5% to 34.0% as courses became longer and harder; authors recommend a more engaging course start | One subject and one university; reasons for dropout inferred, not measured | H1 |
| S3 | Uygun, Cesur, Karahan & Mikhailova — *Discover Education* | 2026 | Qualitative survey | Explore why learners left a MOOC and what they suggest to improve it | 117 of 2,660 non-completers of a general English MOOC (Turkey); 14 open questions; reflexive thematic analysis | External pressures dominate (work f = 191, time 88, academic workload 81); declining motivation f = 38; learners ask for flexible pacing, pause without penalty, milestones, badges and progress bars | Single MOOC; self-reported data; 4.4% response rate; self-selection bias | H1, H2, H3 |
| S4 | Li, Ma & Shi — *Frontiers in Psychology* | 2023 | Meta-analysis | Measure the effect of gamification in formal education | 41 studies / 49 samples, >5,071 participants | Overall g = 0.82; academic performance g = 1.02, engagement g = 0.38; online g = 0.34 vs hybrid g = 0.86 | Very high heterogeneity (I² ≈ 94%); small subgroups; reporting inconsistencies | H3 |
| S5 | Gyedu, Partey, Boafo & Agbo — *SAGE Open* | 2026 | Meta-analysis | Measure the effect on motivation and deep learning in higher education | 22 between-subjects studies in higher education | d = 1.12; offline 2.21 > online 1.08 > hybrid 0.21; effects on engagement smaller than on performance | Only 22 studies; possible publication bias; I² ≈ 99%; mostly short-term studies | H3 |
| S6 | Wahyudi, Budiyanto, Widiastuti & Pambudi — *iJIM* | 2025 | Systematic review | Examine how gamification is used in MOOCs and how it affects engagement and completion | 22 open-access articles (2020–2024) from Scopus | Badges, leaderboards, points, progress bars and challenges most used; mostly positive effects on engagement and retention; in some studies gamification was not a critical driver | 22 articles; no effect sizes; long-term effects under-studied | H3 |
| S7 | Almeida, Kalinowski, Uchôa & Feijó — *Information and Software Technology* | 2023 | Systematic mapping + focus group | Identify which game elements cause which negative effects in education software | 87 papers; focus group with 4 developers | Badges, leaderboards and competition most often linked to negative effects: lack of effect, worse performance, demotivation, cheating | Snowballing not saturated; grey literature excluded; very small focus group | Design risks for XP, badges and leaderboards |
| S8 | Rodrigues et al. — *Int. J. of Educational Technology in Higher Education* | 2022 | Quasi-experimental, longitudinal | Examine how the effects of gamification change over time | 756 programming (CS1) students, Brazil, 14 weeks | Effect declined after ~4 weeks (novelty effect) and partially recovered in weeks 6–10 (familiarization effect); studies shorter than ~12 weeks may mislead | No random assignment; one gamification design; curve shape exploratory | H3; evaluation period |
| S9 | Park & Kim — *JMIR Serious Games* | 2021 | Design study | Develop leaderboard design principles for education | Prior research + analysis of 10 real leaderboards | Combine macro and micro leaderboards; maximise success experiences; low-ranked learners risk demotivation | Principles not tested with learners | Weekly and all-time leaderboards; own position |
| S10 | Uanhoro & Young — *Education Sciences* | 2022 | Quasi-experimental | Examine the effect of badges in an online homework system | 162 first-year physics students, Taiwan | Badges led to earlier submissions (~1.8 days) and more spaced practice; little effect on exam results | Self-selected groups; no long-term follow-up | Achievements |
| S11 | Chen, Wang, Cheng, Chow & Xie — *Future Internet* | 2022 | Quantitative text analysis | Identify what drives MOOC learner satisfaction | 186,738 review sentences from Class Central; deep learning + sentiment analysis | Course quality most discussed (56%); learners satisfied with instructors, dissatisfied with the learning process | One review aggregator; English only; weak classification for some factors | H4 |
| S12 | Nguyen — *Frontiers in Education* | 2022 | Mixed-methods case study | Measure learner satisfaction with Coursera courses | Survey of 120 students + 20 interviews, Vietnam | Low satisfaction (2.39 of 6); assessment and support speed rated lowest | One specialisation; small sample; reporting inconsistencies | H4 |
