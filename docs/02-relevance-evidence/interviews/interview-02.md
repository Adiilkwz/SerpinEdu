### A. Experience with online courses (learners)

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Which online learning platforms have you used? For what purpose? | "I mostly use Udemy and LinkedIn Learning for technical software skills, like learning Autodesk Revit, 3ds Max, or Rhino. I also use Skillshare for more creative things, like architectural sketching or watercolor rendering." |
| A2 | How many online courses have you started in the last two years? How many did you finish? | "I’ve started about 10 courses, and I actually finished 7 or 8 of them. I usually buy a course when I desperately need it for a university project, so I am highly motivated to sit through it to the end." |
| A3 | Think about the last course you did not finish. When and why did you stop? | "I dropped a 'History of Modern Architecture' course on Coursera. It was just reading long texts and answering multiple-choice questions. It felt like reading Wikipedia. As a visual person, if there are no good graphics, diagrams, or interactive models, I lose interest immediately." |
| A4 | What usually makes you continue a course? What makes you stop? | "I continue when the course is project-based. If by the end of the course, I will have a fully rendered 3D house to put in my portfolio, I will definitely finish it. I stop if the instructor is using a software version from 5 years ago and half the buttons they click don't exist anymore." |
| A5 | How do you keep track of your progress in a course? Is it important to you to see it? | "I track my progress by what I can actually model. The platform's progress bar is just a psychological bonus. I prefer seeing a checklist of tools I’ve mastered, like 'You now know how to render lighting'." |
| A6 | What do you like and dislike about the platforms you have used? | "I love when instructors provide the raw project files (.rvt or .dwg) so I can explore their models. I absolutely hate bad video compression. In architectural software, there are hundreds of tiny grey icons. If the video is blurry and I can't see what the instructor clicked, the course is useless." |
| A7 | Did a certificate influence your decision to start or finish a course? | "Not at all. In architecture, nobody cares about a PDF certificate from Udemy. Clients and employers only want to see your portfolio and your actual renders." |

### C. Attitude to gamification

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Have you used apps with points, levels, daily streaks, badges or leaderboards (e.g. Duolingo)? How did they affect you? | "I’ve used Duolingo for Spanish. The gamification is cute, but it feels a bit like a mobile game. It works for 5-minute tasks, but I can't imagine it working for learning complex structural engineering." |
| C2 | Would points (XP) and levels for completed lessons and tests motivate you to continue a course? Why or why not? | "Numbers alone don't motivate me. But if the 'XP' allowed me to visually build something—like, every lesson I complete adds a new floor or aesthetic element to a virtual building on my profile—that would be incredibly satisfying." |
| C3 | A daily streak counts consecutive days of study. Would it motivate you or put pressure on you? What should happen if you miss a day? | "Architecture students are famous for 'charrettes'—pulling all-nighters before a deadline and then sleeping for two days straight. A daily streak would be broken immediately. It would just make me feel guilty. Weekly milestones are much better for project-based learning." |
| C4 | Would you like to see your position on a leaderboard? Weekly, all-time or both? How would you feel if you were far from the top? | "A numeric leaderboard based on speed or points would stress me out. Instead of a leaderboard, I’d love a 'Gallery of the Week' where the platform showcases the best student renders. That kind of visual recognition would make me want to work harder on my assignments." |
| C5 | Which achievements (badges) would be meaningful to you? Which would seem useless? | "Meaningful badges would be skill-specific, like 'BIM Master' or 'Lighting Guru'. Useless badges would be things like 'Logged in on a weekend' or 'Completed 10 quizzes'." |

### D. Closing

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | If you could change one thing in online learning platforms, what would it be? | "Better tools for visual feedback. Right now, if I submit an image of my 3D model, the instructor just writes a text comment. I want a system where peers or teachers can draw directly on my image and leave pinned notes, like in Figma or Miro." |
| D2 | Is there anything else you would like to add? | "The interface of the learning platform itself matters a lot. If the platform looks outdated or poorly designed, creative students will not take the content seriously. Good UI is part of the learning experience." |
