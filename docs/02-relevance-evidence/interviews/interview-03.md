### A. Experience with online courses (learners)

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Which online learning platforms have you used? For what purpose? | "I use Coursera for university-led courses on financial markets and DataCamp for learning Python and SQL for financial analysis." |
| A2 | How many online courses have you started in the last two years? How many did you finish? | "I've started about 6 courses and finished 5 of them. I treat online courses like actual university modules. If I pay for it or put it on my schedule, I finish it." |
| A3 | Think about the last course you did not finish. When and why did you stop? | "I dropped a cryptocurrency trading course a year ago. It turned out to be just hype and marketing without any solid macroeconomic theory or math, so I stopped after the first module." |
| A4 | What usually makes you continue a course? What makes you stop? | "I continue when the course gives me real-world datasets, like actual stock market historical data, to practice on. I stop only if the content is completely irrelevant to my career goals or lacks academic rigor." |
| A5 | How do you keep track of your progress in a course? Is it important to you to see it? | "The progress bar is extremely important to me. I have a very tight schedule, so knowing that a module will take exactly '2 hours and 15 minutes' helps me block out time in my calendar." |
| A6 | What do you like and dislike about the platforms you have used? | "I like clear grading rubrics and auto-graded math quizzes. I dislike mandatory peer reviews. In finance, there is usually one correct mathematical answer, and having a lazy peer grade my complex financial model is frustrating." |
| A7 | Did a certificate influence your decision to start or finish a course? | "Yes, absolutely. Certificates from top universities on Coursera go straight to my LinkedIn and CV. When applying for banking internships, showing completed quantitative courses is a huge advantage." |

### C. Attitude to gamification

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Have you used apps with points, levels, daily streaks, badges or leaderboards (e.g. Duolingo)? How did they affect you? | "I haven't used them much for education, mainly just fitness apps. In a learning context, I think gamification can sometimes distract from the actual material if it's too flashy." |
| C2 | Would points (XP) and levels for completed lessons and tests motivate you to continue a course? Why or why not? | "XP alone wouldn't do much. But if levels were tied to real-world finance roles—like starting as a 'Junior Analyst' and leveling up to 'Portfolio Manager'—that would make the learning path feel more professional and motivating." |
| C3 | A daily streak counts consecutive days of study. Would it motivate you or put pressure on you? What should happen if you miss a day? | "Streaks are good for habit building, but they shouldn't be punishing. If I have university finals, I won't log in for a week. A system that measures 'weekly goals met' rather than daily logins would be much better." |
| C4 | Would you like to see your position on a leaderboard? Weekly, all-time or both? How would you feel if you were far from the top? | "Finance is a highly competitive field, so a leaderboard would definitely push me to work harder. A weekly leaderboard based on quiz scores or model accuracy would be highly motivating." |
| C5 | Which achievements (badges) would be meaningful to you? Which would seem useless? | "Meaningful: 'Flawless Model' or 'Data Cruncher'. Useless: 'Profile Updated' or 'Early Bird'. Badges should represent technical competence." |

### D. Closing

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | If you could change one thing in online learning platforms, what would it be? | "I would add better integration with spreadsheet software. Right now, I have to download a CSV, do the work in Excel, and type the final answer back into the platform. A built-in spreadsheet environment would be amazing." |
| D2 | Is there anything else you would like to add? | "For students who are determined to finish courses, structure is everything. Give us a clear syllabus, predictable workloads, and reliable grading, and we will do the rest." |
