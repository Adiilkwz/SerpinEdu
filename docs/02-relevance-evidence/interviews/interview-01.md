### A. Experience with online courses (learners)

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Which online learning platforms have you used? For what purpose? | "I use Osmosis and Lecturio mostly for visual explanations of anatomy and pathophysiology. Sometimes I use Coursera for general healthcare management courses required by my university." |
| A2 | How many online courses have you started in the last two years? How many did you finish? | "I've started at least 15 different modules or courses. I probably fully finished only 2 of them. I usually just search for a specific topic, like 'cardiac arrhythmias,' watch that one video, and leave." |
| A3 | Think about the last course you did not finish. When and why did you stop? | "It was a pharmacology course on Coursera. I stopped in the second week. The lectures were just a professor reading off slides with endless lists of drug interactions. I have textbooks for that. I dropped it because there were no interactive clinical cases to test my knowledge." |
| A4 | What usually makes you continue a course? What makes you stop? | "I continue if the platform uses 3D models or interactive patient simulations where I have to diagnose based on symptoms. I stop when the content is purely theoretical and text-heavy, or if the quizzes are just multiple-choice definitions rather than applied clinical scenarios." |
| A5 | How do you keep track of your progress in a course? Is it important to you to see it? | "I don't really care about the overall course progress bar. What I care about is tracking my mastery of specific topics. If the system tells me 'You have mastered 80% of Neurology,' that is much more useful than 'Course is 50% complete'." |
| A6 | What do you like and dislike about the platforms you have used? | "I like platforms that offer short, high-yield videos (under 10 minutes) because med students have zero free time. I dislike rigid course structures where I am forced to watch introductory modules before I can access the advanced pathology stuff I actually need for tomorrow's exam." |
| A7 | Did a certificate influence your decision to start or finish a course? | "Not at all. In medicine, no hospital cares about an online certificate from a learning platform. They care about my official university degree, board exam scores, and practical clinical skills." |

### C. Attitude to gamification

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Have you used apps with points, levels, daily streaks, badges or leaderboards (e.g. Duolingo)? How did they affect you? | "I used to use Anki with a gamification add-on for flashcards. It was somewhat motivating at first, but eventually, the pressure of maintaining a streak became more stressful than the actual studying." |
| C2 | Would points (XP) and levels for completed lessons and tests motivate you to continue a course? Why or why not? | "Standard XP feels a bit silly to me at this stage of my education. However, if leveling up represented my 'clinical rank'—like going from Medical Student to Intern, to Resident, to Attending Physician based on the complexity of clinical cases I solved—that would be engaging." |
| C3 | A daily streak counts consecutive days of study. Would it motivate you or put pressure on you? What should happen if you miss a day? | "A daily streak would be a terrible idea for medical students. We have night shifts, clinical rotations, and 14-hour hospital days. If I lose a 50-day streak because I was busy assisting in surgery or sleeping off a night shift, I would probably delete the app out of frustration. Streaks should be based on weekly goals, not daily logins." |
| C4 | Would you like to see your position on a leaderboard? Weekly, all-time or both? How would you feel if you were far from the top? | "Medical school is already highly competitive and toxic when it comes to rankings. I definitely do not want a global leaderboard. It would just cause unnecessary anxiety. I prefer to compete against my own past performance." |
| C5 | Which achievements (badges) would be meaningful to you? Which would seem useless? | "Meaningful achievements would be related to clinical accuracy, like 'Diagnostician: Solved 10 patient cases without errors' or 'Master of Cardiology'. Useless ones are participation trophies like 'Early Bird' or 'Watched 5 videos in a row'." |

### D. Closing

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | If you could change one thing in online learning platforms, what would it be? | "I would completely remove the linear structure. Let users jump straight into complex practical cases and only suggest the theoretical lecture videos if they fail the practical case." |
| D2 | Is there anything else you would like to add? | "If you are designing a system with gamification, make sure there is an option in the settings to completely turn it off. Some days you just want to learn quietly without flashing lights, pop-ups, and point notifications." |
